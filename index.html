Dataset: Climate Change Impact on Agriculture 2024 — six countries (Australia, Brazil, China, France, Nigeria, USA), multiple crop types, soil health indices, fertilizer usage, economic impact, and adaptation strategies.

What the Dashboard Shows
KPI Cards
Top crop, top country, lowest soil health, and highest economic impact at a glance — one number each, front and center on the dashboard.
Countries by Crop Yield
How Australia, Brazil, China, France, Nigeria, and the USA each compare against the global average yield — showing which countries outperform and which fall short.
Net Yield Change per Crop (2024 vs 2023)
Which crops improved year over year and which declined — a direct comparison of 2024 performance against 2023 baselines by crop type.
Adaptation Strategies Share
Breakdown of which farming adaptation methods are most commonly used — drought-resistant crops, irrigation, organic practices, and farms using no strategy at all.
Fertilizer Usage vs Soil Health Index
Whether higher fertilizer use actually corresponds to better soil health — plotted country by country to reveal whether more fertilizer means healthier soil or just higher costs.
Key Findings
+0.71 t/ha vs −0.50 t/ha

Fruits had the highest yield gain from 2023 to 2024, while Wheat had the steepest decline — the widest spread in year-over-year performance across all crop types.

Brazil — 2.36 t/ha

Brazil topped country crop yield at 2.36 t/ha, above the global average. Nigeria and the USA both came in below average, pointing to regional agricultural differences beyond just land area.

$23,059M — Australia

Australia had the highest economic impact despite not having the highest yield — suggesting crop type and market value are bigger drivers of economic output than raw production volume.

France — Soil Health 63.17

France had the lowest soil health index (63.17) while using relatively high fertilizer (50.8 kg/ha), suggesting fertilizer use alone does not protect soil health.

26.9% — No Adaptation

Over a quarter of farms used no adaptation strategy at all. Among those that did, drought-resistant crops (25.1%) were the most common method — a sign of climate pressure on farming decisions.
